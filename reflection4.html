Nuclear War: The Rising Risk and How We Stop It

The article source “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk and How We Stop It,” written by W.J. Hennigan, and published in the New York Times, tells the reader about the risks of nuclear war and the risks of countries using nuclear weaponry. The article discusses the damage such weapons can and will course, and the risks associated with their usage, most notably that millions of innocent people could get caught in the crossfire, getting seriously hurt or even killed should there be a nuclear war. The part of the article that impacted me the most was the section with examples about nuclear danger as not just a historical relic from the events that took place in the Cold War, 1945, but as reflective of possible risks within today’s world. The article discusses how nuclear war or the threat of nuclear war can be shaped and how nuclear war could easily emerged.

The main argument found in “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk and How We Stop” is that the risk of nuclear war and that the damage such a war would cause is unprecedented. The article claims that nuclear danger persists partly because people assume that politicians and scientists are handling the risks when this is likely not the case. The article suggests that a meaningful change with respect to the risks would require political pressure and a cultural shift aiming to achieve a long-term global survival instead of a short-term goal of preventing the immediate outbreak of nuclear war.

The highlights positioned in the article help the reader understand the steps necessary to reduce nuclear risk of nuclear war, such as renewing the agreements on arms-control, the warnings and promoting global cooperations. The part of the article I liked was how it describes the possible dangers we want to avoid by acknowledging them despite the insist on nuclear wars being preventable. It also facts how the risks were our primary concerns that the Biden administration was mainly aware of and that we should never forget the consequences that can happen to our home planet and its people, including the numerous amounts of death that can happen. Even though the history of the nuclear war was left in the past and put behind us all, we must keep all this information this in our minds and use the facts from the article about the possibilities of another nuclear war, so we can ensure that something like does not happen ever again as long as we heed the warnings.

I find a similarity to this because we want to keep our world safe like we do with keeping it clean and healthy on eventful days such as Earth Day and spring cleaning. Reading the article has taught me that a nuclear war isn’t evitable, but a devastating threat which requires a couple of solutions for us to use to solve the situation peacefully and prevent violent disasters from spreading across the globe. The primary challenge we may expect to find in a situation such as this is to convince other societies on Earth that we should care before being allowing ourselves to be forced by more than just one catastrophe that can often occur in our very lives.
