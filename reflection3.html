Motherhood in Crisis

The graphic novel Motherhood in Crisis tells the story of four women who have experienced tremendous hardship in Sierra Leone. The main focus of the novel is how being pregnant and being a mother in this part of the world is very dangerous.

The main focus of the novel is how being pregnant and being a mother in this part of the world is very dangerous.

The text and the visual elements combine to emphasize the scale of the danger associated with giving birth, while also focusing on the story of individuals.

The novel begins with an overview of the dangers of giving birth. It mentions facts, using white text against a black background.

The facts are bleak and the visual elements reinforce this: untrained birth attendants oversee births, which is life-threatening for mothers.

The text also emphasizes that prolonged labor is the main reason for problems, including still-births and obstetric fistula, which damage to the woman’s body.

From this point, the text moves to the individual women’s stories, personalizing the terrible reality. The individual stories emphasize graphics, stressing the physical suffering of women in labor. The first image for Memunatu’s story, for example, showcases the poverty that she lives in. The narrative, presenting her voice in first-person, stresses that she could not afford to travel to hospital when she was in labor because it was too far away.
